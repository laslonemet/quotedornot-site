01 Why now

The shortlist moved. Most firms haven't noticed.

The first call moved

Buyers who used to search Google for "top firms in your niche" now ask an AI assistant — and act on the handful of names it gives them. Google traffic to B2B sites is measurably dropping. The questions didn't disappear; they went somewhere else.

There is no page two

A search result ranks ten links. An AI answer names a few firms and moves on. If you're not one of them, you don't rank lower — you're absent. Absence doesn't show up in your analytics.

The loss is invisible

No impression, no click, no bounce rate. The shortlist formed without you and the deal closed somewhere else. The only way to see it is to ask the models the same questions your buyers ask — and record what comes back.

06 FAQ

Fair questions.

Which AI models do you test?
Four independent model families: Qwen, DeepSeek, Hermes, and MiniMax. Each is trained by a different team, on different data, with different methods — which is exactly why we test more than one. We record every answer verbatim, so you can read precisely what each model says about you and about the competitors it names.
Is this just SEO with a new name?
No. SEO competes for ranked links on a results page. AI assistants answer with a short list of names — there is no page two. Some fundamentals overlap (a clear, crawlable site; consistent company information), but the surface, the mechanics, and the failure modes are different. This audit measures the AI surface specifically, and the fix list is written for it.
What if we're already visible?
Then the report documents it. You'll know which questions you win, which you lose, and which competitors are gaining ground. It also becomes your baseline for measuring change over time. And the guarantee still applies: if we find nothing actionable, you pay nothing.
Why four models instead of one?
Because buyers don't all use the same assistant, and models disagree. One model's answer is an anecdote; four independent answers are a pattern. When three of four models name a competitor and none name you, that's a signal worth acting on — and one a single-model check would never show you.
What happens after the audit?
You implement the fix list yourself, or hand it to your web or marketing team — it's written for practitioners, not executives. After 90 days, a $149 re-audit runs the same questions against the same models and measures what moved. There's no retainer and nothing else to buy.
